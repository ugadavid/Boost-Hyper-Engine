# Boost'English Corpus Cartography

## 1. Context

This document belongs to the `experiment/bhe-be` branch.

Boost'English is used here as a situated pedagogical corpus: a set of real teaching bricks, written by real teachers, with real constraints, local decisions, uneven strengths, and visible production traces. The goal of this cartography is not to judge the bricks and not to derive BHE architecture too quickly.

The immediate goal is simpler:

```txt
observe
describe
map
```

This corpus memory should help future BHE-BE POCs by making the landscape easier to see before interpreting it.

## 3. First Descriptive Patterns

Several bricks follow a recurring learner-facing rhythm:

```txt
I start
-> I learn
-> I practise
-> I say
-> I do
-> I share
-> I want more
-> For teachers
```

This template is visible across many grammar bricks, but the pedagogical use of the template varies.

Some bricks are mostly grammar-focus pathways:

- observe or notice a form;
- receive or reconstruct a rule;
- practise with controlled exercises;
- finish with a timed quiz;
- reuse the form in speaking or writing.

Other bricks use similar interaction shapes for a different function:

- reflective questionnaires with no real score;
- confidence building;
- strategy awareness;
- preparation for speaking;
- teacher-mediated classroom extension.

The grammar bricks are not all identical. Some lean toward content accumulation and repeated practice, while others have stronger guided discovery signals. `Countable & Uncountable Nouns` is especially visible as an observe-and-reason path. `Question-like` activities also appear in several roles: assessment, noticing, reflection, and comprehension.

The "For teachers" sections are often pedagogically rich. They frequently move beyond the learner-facing exercises into classroom orchestration: pair work, games, discussion, guessing activities, maps, story writing, pronunciation practice, or teacher-led variations.

## 4. Surprises

The first surprise is that weak-looking or mechanically familiar interaction shapes can carry different pedagogical intentions. A quiz may be an assessment, but it may also be a reflective positioning tool.

The second surprise is how often oral activation appears, even inside grammar bricks. Many bricks add a voicework stage or a speaking/writing extension after controlled practice.

The third surprise is the contrast between highly templated structure and local inventiveness. Several bricks share the same macro-sequence, but the notable moves live in small details: a delayed explanation after a matching task, a reasoning question about countability, image-based thinking, a map-based route task, or a teacher extension that transforms grammar into a game.

The fourth surprise is the practical design layer visible in teacher comments: score visibility, media placement, copyright, audio hosting, hidden teacher sections, platform constraints, and unclear interaction feasibility. The corpus is not only pedagogical content; it is also a record of negotiation with an implementation environment.

## 5. Temporary Conclusion

This document is intended as corpus memory, not theory.

The corpus shows recurring pedagogical families, but it does not yet justify a strong architectural conclusion. It does suggest that future BHE-BE POCs should keep looking at the gap between:

```txt
visible interaction shape
and
pedagogical function
```

Some bricks are high-potential because they reveal strong orchestration or unusual cognitive movement. Other bricks are valuable precisely because they are more conventional: they offer contrast and help identify where a concept may stop being useful.

The safest next use of this cartography is to select future POCs by contrast, not only by elegance.
